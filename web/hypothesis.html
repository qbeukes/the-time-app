Temporal Dislocation: A Multiscale Entrainment Hypothesis

A Proposed Research Construct within the 12 Temporal Resonance Architecture

Working status

Temporal Dislocation is proposed as a research construct describing persistent or recurrent misalignment between an individual's endogenous biological timing, behavioural timing, subjective experience of change, and naturally occurring environmental temporal cycles.

The term Temporal Dislocation Syndrome (TDS) may eventually describe a clinically meaningful phenotype if research demonstrates a sufficiently coherent and reproducible cluster of symptoms, biomarkers, functional impairments, and responses to intervention.

At the present stage, however, Temporal Dislocation is the scientifically preferable term. Calling it a syndrome before validation would imply a degree of clinical establishment that does not yet exist.

The central proposition is:

Human wellbeing may depend not merely on obtaining sufficient sleep or maintaining a regular schedule, but on maintaining coherent phase relationships across multiple endogenous and environmental rhythms.

12TRA proposes that some forms of modern dysregulation can therefore be understood not simply as disorders occurring in time, but as disorders of an organism's relationship to time.

1. The central idea

Living organisms are temporal systems.

They do not merely exist while time passes. Biological processes actively anticipate recurring environmental change.

Human physiology contains endogenous oscillators controlling or influencing:

  • sleep and wakefulness,
  • hormone secretion,
  • body temperature,
  • metabolism,
  • immune function,
  • cardiovascular physiology,
  • cognition,
  • mood,
  • feeding,
  • physical performance,
  • cellular activity.

The circadian system is the best-established example.

Under constant conditions, the human circadian oscillator does not remain exactly synchronized to a 24-hour civil clock. External signals—especially the solar light-dark cycle—continually entrain it to environmental time. Light is overwhelmingly the dominant known human circadian zeitgeber. Feeding, exercise and behavioural schedules can also contribute to temporal organization.

Consequently:

biological time is relational rather than merely chronological.

The organism repeatedly establishes a phase relationship between its internal oscillators and periodic events in its environment.

Temporal Dislocation extends this established principle from:

circadian alignment

toward the broader question of:

multiscale temporal alignment.

2. Established scientific foundation

2.1 Circadian entrainment

The mammalian suprachiasmatic nucleus acts as a principal circadian pacemaker and helps coordinate peripheral clocks throughout the body. Biological clocks are therefore distributed rather than represented by one simple clock.

The concept of entrainment is already fundamental to chronobiology.

An endogenous oscillator and an environmental oscillator become coupled so that their periods agree while maintaining a characteristic phase relationship.

This immediately provides an important conceptual distinction:

A person can obey civil clock time while remaining biologically misaligned.

For example:

  • wake at 06:00,
  • eat at prescribed times,
  • begin work at 08:00,
  • sleep at 23:00,

while their underlying biological night, melatonin rhythm, sleep propensity and peripheral metabolic clocks remain differently phased.

This is not speculative. It is the foundation of circadian-misalignment research.

2.2 Circadian misalignment

Circadian misalignment already describes mismatch among sleep-wake behaviour, endogenous biological timing, feeding rhythms, central clocks and peripheral oscillators.

Experimental and epidemiological research connects circadian disruption with changes involving metabolism, cardiovascular physiology, mood and health risk.

Temporal Dislocation therefore should not compete with circadian misalignment.

Instead:

Circadian misalignment is proposed as one experimentally established subclass of a potentially broader phenomenon of temporal dislocation.

This distinction is crucial.

3. Social time versus biological time

A particularly important precedent is social jetlag.

Social jetlag describes discrepancy between biological timing and schedules imposed by work, school and social organization.

This gives us an established three-part distinction:

Astronomical/environmental time

Cycles generated by the physical environment.

Examples:

  • solar day,
  • day/night illumination,
  • seasons,
  • lunar illumination,
  • tidal/gravitational periodicities.

Biological time

Endogenous oscillatory physiology.

Examples:

  • circadian phase,
  • melatonin timing,
  • body-temperature rhythms,
  • peripheral cellular clocks.

Social/civil time

Human-created temporal coordination systems.

Examples:

  • clock time,
  • work schedules,
  • school schedules,
  • weekends,
  • time zones,
  • calendar months,
  • deadlines.

These three temporal layers can coincide.

But they do not necessarily do so.

Temporal Dislocation begins with the proposition that increasing divergence among them may matter physiologically and psychologically.

4. 12TRA's extension: multiscale temporal organization

The novel 12TRA proposition is that entrainment should be investigated across multiple scales, rather than assuming that only the approximately 24-hour cycle is biologically meaningful.

Earth presents organisms with numerous recurring geophysical cycles:

  • approximately 12.4-hour tidal rhythms,
  • approximately 24-hour solar rhythms,
  • approximately 14.77-day semilunar rhythms,
  • approximately 29.53-day synodic lunar rhythms,
  • annual seasonal rhythms.

Biological clocks corresponding to several of these periodicities are known across many species. Lunar and tidal timing systems are especially well established in marine organisms.

This leads to the first major 12TRA research question:

Does human temporal organization contain biologically meaningful oscillatory structure beyond the circadian and seasonal scales?

This is a legitimate empirical question.

5. The human lunar question

Historically, lunar claims about humans have been burdened by folklore, confirmation bias and weak methodology.

Many claims regarding:

  • crime,
  • psychiatric admission,
  • accidents,
  • childbirth,
  • aggression,
  • emergency presentations,

have failed replication or produced inconsistent findings.

Any serious 12TRA project therefore has to explicitly reject the simplistic proposition:

"The full moon causes human behaviour."

That is not the hypothesis.

The scientifically interesting question is considerably narrower:

Do humans possess weak, variable or partially masked circa-lunar biological oscillations capable of entrainment, relative coordination or phase modulation?

There is currently evidence sufficient to make this question scientifically interesting, but not sufficient to consider the matter settled.

A particularly relevant 2026 review by Helfrich-Förster and Wehr argues from evolutionary and physiological evidence that humans may retain a circa(semi)lunar timing mechanism. The authors explicitly characterize the proposition as a hypothesis and discuss moonlight as a potential primary zeitgeber and gravimetric lunar cycles as a possible secondary one.

Earlier reviews likewise describe suggestive lunar periodicities while emphasizing unresolved mechanisms and methodological limitations.

Thus 12TRA can legitimately propose:

Human circalunar entrainment is an open experimental problem.

It should not claim:

Human circalunar entrainment has already been established.

6. Gravity: the strongest and weakest part of the theory

Gravity requires particularly disciplined language.

It is unquestionably biologically relevant.

Cells and organisms evolved under Earth's gravitational environment. Mechanotransduction allows mechanical forces to influence cellular signalling and gene expression. Specialized biological structures directly detect gravity, and altered gravitational loading affects many physiological systems.

Spaceflight and simulated microgravity can also alter sleep, circadian variables and clock-related gene expression, although multiple confounding conditions coexist in spaceflight—including abnormal light exposure, workload, isolation and altered schedules.

What has not been established is the much stronger proposition that:

small ordinary lunar variations in gravitational force directly regulate human psychology or behaviour.

Therefore the research program should separate three propositions.

Proposition G1 — established

Gravity and mechanical loading have biological consequences.

Proposition G2 — established outside humans / plausible generally

Lunar and solar gravitational geometry generates predictable geophysical periodicities, most visibly tides, and organisms can evolve rhythms associated with tidal environments.

Proposition G3 — unestablished

The comparatively subtle terrestrial gravimetric lunar cycle serves as a physiologically meaningful human zeitgeber.

G3 is a research hypothesis, not an assumption.

This distinction protects the entire project scientifically.

7. A reconstructed definition of Temporal Dislocation

We can now define the construct more precisely.

Temporal Dislocation

A state of reduced temporal coherence in which one or more endogenous physiological, behavioural, cognitive or experiential rhythms maintain maladaptive phase relationships with other endogenous rhythms or with recurrent environmental cycles relevant to organismic regulation.

The important words are:

Reduced temporal coherence

The problem is not necessarily that a particular rhythm is absent.

The rhythms may individually remain functional while losing their normal relationships.

Phase relationship

Two oscillators need not peak simultaneously to be synchronized.

Healthy organization may require a stable phase angle rather than simultaneity.

For example:

sleep onset, melatonin rise and core-body-temperature minimum normally occur at different times.

Coherence means appropriate sequencing.

Multioscillator

Temporal Dislocation need not involve only a central clock.

It may involve:

  • central versus peripheral clocks,
  • sleep versus metabolic timing,
  • behavioural versus circadian timing,
  • circadian versus longer-period oscillations,
  • biological versus environmental timing.

8. Temporal coherence versus temporal regularity

This distinction is fundamental to 12TRA.

Regularity is not necessarily resonance.

A person can perform something at precisely the same artificial clock time every day and still be biologically misaligned.

Conversely, a schedule that moves slightly with environmental conditions could conceivably maintain a more stable biological phase relationship.

Therefore:

Regularity = repeated behaviour according to an external clock.

Entrainment = oscillator coupling.

Phase alignment = appropriate temporal relationship.

Temporal coherence = coordinated organization among multiple relevant rhythms.

Temporal resonance, within 12TRA terminology, can be provisionally defined as:

A state in which endogenous and environmentally coupled temporal processes maintain stable, adaptive phase relationships across relevant timescales.

This makes "resonance" operational rather than mystical.

9. Levels of Temporal Dislocation

A useful model is to separate several levels.

Level I — Circadian dislocation

Mismatch involving the approximately 24-hour system.

Examples:

  • shift work,
  • jet lag,
  • irregular sleep,
  • nocturnal light exposure,
  • inappropriate feeding times,
  • daylight-saving transitions.

This level is already extensively supported by chronobiology.

Level II — Internal temporal dislocation

Different endogenous systems become desynchronized from one another.

For example:

SCN phase ≠ peripheral metabolic phase ≠ sleep timing ≠ feeding timing

This is closely related to established concepts of internal desynchronization and circadian misalignment.

Level III — Social temporal dislocation

Mismatch between endogenous timing and externally imposed schedules.

biological time ≠ institutional time

Social jetlag is an established example.

Level IV — Environmental temporal dislocation

An organism receives weakened, contradictory or artificially altered natural zeitgebers.

Potential contributors include:

  • weak daylight exposure,
  • artificial light at night,
  • indoor living,
  • temperature-controlled environments,
  • irregular food schedules,
  • reduced seasonality,
  • inconsistent activity schedules.

Modern environmental conditions can indeed weaken or alter natural temporal cues, especially light.

Level V — Infradian temporal dislocation

The 12TRA extension.

Possible mismatch involving rhythms longer than 24 hours:

  • semilunar,
  • lunar,
  • menstrual,
  • other multiday physiological oscillations,
  • seasonal rhythms.

This level requires significantly more research.

Level VI — Experiential temporal dislocation

This is the psychological component.

An individual may experience:

  • days as undifferentiated,
  • weak perception of transition,
  • loss of temporal orientation toward change,
  • disrupted anticipation,
  • difficulty closing one phase and entering another,
  • chronic sensation of stasis,
  • mismatch between subjective readiness and environmental demands.

This layer should initially be treated as a psychometric hypothesis, not a medical symptom cluster.

It is nevertheless potentially important because biological clocks evolved partly to enable anticipation rather than mere reaction.

10. Change is central to the theory

This recovers an important part of the original 12TRA formulation.

Temporal systems exist because environments change predictably.

A biological oscillator provides adaptive advantage because it allows the organism to prepare for change before the change occurs.

Thus:

Biological timekeeping is fundamentally an architecture for anticipating recurring change.

From this perspective, Temporal Dislocation can be framed as:

impairment in the organism's ability to maintain appropriate temporal relationships with predictable processes of change.

This is much stronger scientifically than saying merely that someone is "out of sync with the Moon."

11. The 12TRA interpretation

12TRA adds a phenomenological layer.

Rather than treating a lunar month as an arbitrary set of numbered civil-calendar days, it uses the physically observable lunar cycle as a repeating environmental frame.

The underlying research question becomes:

Does explicit orientation toward a naturally occurring geophysical cycle improve temporal awareness, behavioural organization or biological coherence compared with orientation solely toward civil-calendar time?

That question can be tested even if no direct lunar physiological effect exists.

This is strategically important.

12TRA may potentially operate through several different mechanisms:

Mechanism A — direct biological entrainment

A natural cycle directly entrains or modulates physiology.

Mechanism B — indirect environmental entrainment

The cycle changes light or other environmental properties which subsequently alter physiology.

Mechanism C — behavioural entrainment

Awareness of the cycle changes sleep, exercise, activity, planning or social behaviour.

Mechanism D — cognitive temporal scaffolding

A natural cycle creates recurring transition markers that improve temporal orientation, anticipation and reflection.

Mechanism E — combined effects

Several weak effects combine into a measurable outcome.

The research should identify which mechanism, if any, is operating rather than assume one beforehand.

12. Temporal Dislocation is not simply "losing track of time"

This deserves explicit emphasis.

A temporally dislocated person might know:

  • today's date,
  • the exact time,
  • their schedule,
  • every deadline.

Their chronological orientation can be completely intact.

The proposed disturbance instead concerns:

phase relationships between lived, biological and environmental time.

Hence:

chronological accuracy ≠ temporal coherence

This is one of the most important conceptual contributions of the model.

13. Proposed phenotype

If Temporal Dislocation proves to be measurable, it may manifest across several domains.

These are candidate variables, not diagnostic criteria.

Sleep/circadian

  • unstable sleep timing,
  • high social jetlag,
  • difficulty awakening,
  • delayed or advanced phase,
  • poor sleep continuity,
  • variable alertness.

Physiological

Potentially measurable through:

  • melatonin phase,
  • core or distal temperature,
  • resting heart-rate rhythms,
  • heart-rate variability,
  • cortisol,
  • glucose timing,
  • activity rhythms.

Cognitive

Potential candidates:

  • attentional variability,
  • reaction-time variability,
  • executive-function fluctuation,
  • impaired temporal estimation,
  • weak anticipatory planning.

Psychological

Potential candidates:

  • mood variability,
  • subjective temporal fragmentation,
  • diminished continuity between past/present/future,
  • perceived temporal pressure,
  • difficulty recognizing transitions.

Behavioural

Potential candidates:

  • irregular meals,
  • irregular activity,
  • inconsistent work/rest boundaries,
  • high discrepancy between workdays and free days,
  • reduced exposure to environmental timing cues.

Again, these should enter the study as variables, not symptoms presumed to belong to a disease.

14. The Temporal Coherence Model

We can formalize the architecture as interacting temporal layers:

Geophysical oscillators ↓ solar / lunar / seasonal / gravitational / environmental periodicity

Environmental signals ↓ light / temperature / activity opportunity / food availability / mechanical environment

Biological oscillators ↓ circadian / ultradian / infradian / seasonal systems

Behavioural rhythms ↓ sleep / feeding / movement / work / social activity

Cognitive temporal models ↓ anticipation / planning / memory / perceived transitions

Identity and behaviour over time

The conventional biomedical model usually studies portions of this architecture.

12TRA asks whether coherence across the architecture itself should become the object of measurement.

15. The original 12TRA causal proposition

An early conceptual formulation can now be made considerably more rigorous.

Instead of:

gravity → psychology

the scientifically defensible version is:

geophysical cycles → environmental temporal signals → biological entrainment → physiological organization → behaviour and cognition → subjective lived experience

with possible feedback loops between each layer.

This preserves the original intuition that psychology is downstream from physical temporal structure without making an unsupported direct causal claim.

16. A central concept: phase coherence

The most promising mathematical formulation may not be "amount of lunar influence."

It may be phase coherence.

For each measurable rhythm (i), estimate:

  • period,
  • phase,
  • amplitude,
  • stability.

Then investigate phase relationships:

  • sleep ↔ melatonin,
  • melatonin ↔ activity,
  • feeding ↔ circadian phase,
  • temperature ↔ sleep,
  • mood ↔ circadian phase,
  • behavioural rhythm ↔ environmental light,
  • candidate infradian rhythm ↔ lunar phase.

A hypothetical Temporal Coherence Index (TCI) could eventually combine normalized measures of these relationships.

The research question would become:

Does lower multivariate temporal coherence predict poorer physiological, cognitive or psychological outcomes?

This is substantially more falsifiable than a broad claim about "being in resonance."

17. Candidate core hypotheses

H1 — Circadian validation hypothesis

Participants with greater established circadian misalignment will show lower Temporal Coherence Index scores.

This provides convergent validation.

H2 — Internal coherence hypothesis

Greater phase instability among sleep, activity, temperature and endocrine markers will correlate with poorer functional outcomes.

H3 — environmental-cue hypothesis

Greater exposure to strong natural temporal cues, especially daytime light and dark nights, will predict improved biological phase stability.

This is strongly anchored in existing chronobiology.

H4 — social-dislocation hypothesis

Greater divergence between biological phase and institutional schedule will correlate with poorer temporal coherence.

This overlaps with social-jetlag research and provides another validation anchor.

H5 — infradian periodicity hypothesis

Longitudinal individual-level data will reveal reproducible physiological or behavioural periodicities in approximately semilunar/lunar frequency bands in at least a subset of participants.

This is exploratory.

H6 — lunar phase-locking hypothesis

Some detected infradian oscillations will maintain non-random phase relationships with one or more lunar/geophysical cycles.

This is substantially more speculative.

H7 — gravimetric hypothesis

After controlling for lunar illumination and conventional environmental variables, measurable biological variables will show reproducible associations with calculated gravimetric periodicities.

This is a high-risk exploratory hypothesis.

Failure to support H7 must not invalidate H1-H6 or the broader Temporal Dislocation construct.

H8 — temporal orientation intervention hypothesis

An intervention explicitly orienting participants toward recurring natural cycles will improve subjective temporal coherence and behavioural regularity relative to an appropriate control condition.

H9 — 12TRA-specific hypothesis

A 12TRA-based temporal scaffolding intervention will produce greater improvement in predefined temporal-coherence outcomes than a structurally equivalent civil-calendar planning intervention.

This is the critical test of whether 12TRA contributes something beyond ordinary scheduling.

18. Falsifiability

For the model to be fundable, it must be possible for experiments to prove portions of it wrong.

Examples:

If no reproducible infradian structure appears after adequate longitudinal measurement, the proposed lunar component is weakened.

If infradian rhythms exist but show no relationship to lunar/geophysical cycles, an endogenous multiday oscillator may exist without lunar entrainment.

If lunar correlations disappear after controlling for nocturnal illumination, moonlight rather than gravity becomes the more plausible mechanism.

If 12TRA performs no better than a normal calendar intervention, its special temporal architecture receives no empirical support.

If subjective "resonance" improves but biomarkers do not, 12TRA may function primarily as a cognitive or behavioural temporal scaffold.

If biological markers improve without subjective effects, an alternative physiological interpretation becomes necessary.

Negative results therefore refine the architecture rather than being explained away.

That attitude should be explicit in every funding document.

19. Measurement strategy

A serious study should favor dense longitudinal within-person data.

This is particularly important because weak periodic phenomena can disappear when people with different phases are averaged together.

Potential measurements:

Passive

  • actigraphy,
  • sleep timing,
  • ambient light exposure,
  • skin temperature,
  • heart rate,
  • HRV,
  • physical activity,
  • phone activity,
  • location-derived sunrise/sunset exposure.

Active

  • sleep diary,
  • mood,
  • energy,
  • subjective temporal coherence,
  • perceived transition,
  • cognitive testing,
  • meal timing,
  • exercise timing.

Laboratory subset

  • dim-light melatonin onset (DLMO),
  • cortisol profiles,
  • core body temperature,
  • polysomnography,
  • metabolic markers.

Environmental

  • sunrise/sunset,
  • photoperiod,
  • weather,
  • artificial light exposure,
  • lunar illumination,
  • lunar altitude,
  • lunar phase,
  • Earth-Moon-Sun geometry,
  • tidal/gravitational model variables.

20. The essential analytic principle: individual time series

A major weakness of many popular "moon studies" is asking questions such as:

Are hospital admissions higher during a full moon?

That aggregates heterogeneous people and looks for a crude population event.

The 12TRA question is different:

Does an individual's physiology exhibit a reproducible oscillation, and if so, what environmental oscillator best predicts its phase?

This calls for:

  • repeated measures,
  • spectral analysis,
  • cosinor analysis,
  • wavelet analysis,
  • phase-locking statistics,
  • cross-correlation,
  • mixed-effects models,
  • individual-specific models,
  • preregistered periodicities,
  • correction for multiple comparisons.

The unit of discovery should initially be the longitudinal individual, followed by population-level estimation of how common each pattern is.

21. Avoiding the lunar-correlation trap

Lunar research is exceptionally susceptible to false positives because many overlapping periodicities exist.

A rigorous study must preregister candidate periods.

At minimum distinguish:

  • synodic month: ~29.53 days,
  • anomalistic month: ~27.55 days,
  • tropical/sidereal-related periods around ~27.3 days,
  • semilunar periods around ~14.7 days,
  • circadian,
  • weekly/social periodicities,
  • menstrual periodicity where applicable,
  • seasonal changes.

Otherwise, sufficiently flexible statistical analysis can manufacture apparent lunar correlations.

This methodological discipline would itself distinguish a serious 12TRA program from popular lunar research.

22. Civil calendar as experimental variable

One of the most interesting 12TRA propositions does not require any unproven gravity effect.

Civil calendars organize time into abstractions:

  • named months of unequal duration,
  • seven-day weeks,
  • institutionally defined work cycles.

These abstractions are socially indispensable.

The research question is not whether calendars are "bad."

Instead:

Does exclusive cognitive organization around civil-calendar units weaken awareness of natural environmental periodicity or increase behavioural conflict with biological timing?

This can be experimentally investigated.

A 12TRA intervention could expose participants simultaneously to:

  • conventional civil date,
  • solar position/daylight information,
  • lunar-cycle position,
  • personal biological timing,
  • behavioural phase information.

Its effect can then be compared with conventional calendar planning.

23. Re-synchronization

Within the model, Temporal Resynchronization would describe increasing coherence among relevant timing systems.

That could occur through:

  • stronger morning/daytime light,
  • reduced inappropriate nocturnal light,
  • regular sleep,
  • phase-appropriate meals,
  • timed exercise,
  • reduced social jetlag,
  • greater natural environmental exposure,
  • deliberate awareness of longer environmental cycles.

Some of those interventions already have scientific foundations in circadian medicine.

Others—particularly lunar orientation—remain experimental.

12TRA can therefore be conceptualized not initially as a therapy but as a:

Temporal Orientation and Entrainment Framework.

24. Why the 12 archetypes should remain outside the first biological claim

The archetypal layer of 12TRA should initially be studied separately from the physiological entrainment hypothesis.

The first-generation research should ask:

  1. Do multiscale patterns exist?
  2. Are they environmentally phase-related?
  3. Can temporal coherence be quantified?
  4. Can temporal orientation modify it?

Only afterward should the 12 archetype system be examined as a psychological or phenomenological interpretation of repeated phases.

This sequencing prevents reviewers from dismissing a potentially valid chronobiological research program because it is bundled prematurely with an interpretive archetype framework.

The archetypes can later become a separate hypothesis:

Do recurring temporal phases acquire reproducible phenomenological or behavioural characteristics?

That is experimentally testable, but it should not be assumed beforehand.

25. Proposed research programme

Phase 0 — Construct development

Develop:

  • Temporal Dislocation definition,
  • Temporal Coherence model,
  • Temporal Coherence Scale,
  • objective Temporal Coherence Index,
  • preregistered mechanistic hypotheses.

Perform expert review with:

  • chronobiologists,
  • sleep researchers,
  • neuroscientists,
  • psychologists,
  • biostatisticians,
  • physicists/geophysicists.

Phase I — Observational discovery

A long-duration cohort.

Ideal duration:

6–12 months, preferably longer.

Why?

A 29.53-day phenomenon produces only about twelve observations of its full cycle per year.

Short studies are intrinsically underpowered for robust circalunar inference.

Collect dense wearable and ecological-momentary-assessment data.

Primary objective:

identify individual temporal structure without imposing 12TRA categories.

Phase II — Replication

Repeat candidate periodic effects in an independent cohort.

Predefine:

  • frequencies,
  • phase relationships,
  • outcomes,
  • exclusions,
  • statistical thresholds.

Only effects that replicate progress.

Phase III — Mechanism discrimination

Separate candidate influences.

For example:

Moonlight hypothesis

Compare natural nocturnal light exposure with lunar phase.

Cognitive-expectation hypothesis

Blind participants to lunar information.

Gravimetric hypothesis

Use calculated gravitational variables independent of visible lunar phase.

Social-cycle hypothesis

Control weekday/weekend and occupational schedules.

Menstrual confounding

Model participant-specific menstrual cycles independently.

Phase IV — intervention study

Randomize participants to something like:

Group A conventional time-management/calendar intervention.

Group B circadian-alignment intervention.

Group C circadian alignment + 12TRA natural-cycle temporal orientation.

Possible outcomes:

  • sleep stability,
  • social jetlag,
  • DLMO phase,
  • subjective coherence,
  • mood,
  • executive performance,
  • adherence,
  • wellbeing.

This determines whether 12TRA adds anything beyond established circadian hygiene.

26. Clinical caution

Until validated, Temporal Dislocation must not be used to replace established diagnoses such as:

  • circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorders,
  • insomnia,
  • depression,
  • bipolar disorder,
  • ADHD,
  • dissociative disorders,
  • neurocognitive conditions.

Nor should conventional treatment be replaced by "resynchronization."

Temporal Dislocation should initially function as a research phenotype spanning temporal organization, not as a diagnosis.

If strong evidence eventually identifies:

  1. a reproducible symptom cluster,
  2. objective temporal biomarkers,
  3. functional impairment,
  4. discriminant validity,
  5. longitudinal stability,
  6. intervention response,

then the term Temporal Dislocation Syndrome could be reconsidered.

27. Proposed concise definition for publications

Temporal Dislocation

Temporal Dislocation is a hypothesized state of reduced multiscale temporal coherence characterized by unstable or maladaptive phase relationships among endogenous biological rhythms, behavioural schedules, subjective temporal organization, and relevant environmental cycles.

Temporal Resonance

Temporal Resonance is the proposed condition in which these systems maintain stable and adaptive phase relationships across relevant timescales.

Temporal Resynchronization

Temporal Resynchronization is the process by which disrupted temporal relationships return toward a more stable and adaptive configuration.

28. Research-board formulation

The project should not be pitched as:

"We believe people are becoming sick because they are disconnected from the Moon's gravity."

That framing would overstate the evidence and probably terminate serious review.

The scientifically defensible pitch is:

Modern chronobiology has established that human physiology depends upon entrainment to recurring environmental time cues and that misalignment among biological, behavioural and social time can impair function and health. Most human research has concentrated on approximately 24-hour circadian organization. The proposed programme investigates whether temporal coherence can be generalized into a measurable multiscale construct, whether reproducible infradian organization exists in human longitudinal physiology and behaviour, whether any such rhythms maintain phase relationships with naturally occurring geophysical cycles, and whether explicit natural-cycle temporal orientation can improve measurable temporal coherence.

That is a genuine research programme.

29. The deeper 12TRA proposition

Once stripped of claims that outrun the evidence, the original insight remains intact:

Humans are embedded in nested physical cycles of change.

Our bodies contain mechanisms that predict recurring change.

Modern societies increasingly permit behaviour to occur independently of many of the environmental signals under which those mechanisms evolved.

We already know that sufficiently strong separation between behavioural/social timing and circadian timing produces measurable misalignment.

The unresolved question is:

How far does that principle extend?

Does human temporal organization meaningfully extend beyond circadian synchronization?

Are there weak multiday rhythms normally obscured by artificial environments?

Can temporal coherence be quantified across biological, behavioural and subjective domains?

Can greater awareness of natural temporal structure improve human regulation?

And, most distinctively:

Is some portion of what we interpret as psychological dysregulation actually downstream of disturbed temporal organization?

That is the central research question underlying Temporal Dislocation within 12TRA.

It is not presently an established scientific fact.

But it is specific enough to generate falsifiable hypotheses, measurable variables, competing mechanisms and controlled experiments.

That is what makes it researchable.
