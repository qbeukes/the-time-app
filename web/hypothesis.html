Introduction

1. The 12TRA Research Proposition

12TRA begins with a simple observation:

Human beings do not merely live in time. We are biological systems embedded within recurring physical cycles.

Day and night, seasons, lunar illumination, gravitational cycles, temperature, activity, sleep, feeding, hormonal rhythms, and social schedules all create overlapping temporal structures.

Modern chronobiology already establishes the importance of some of these relationships, particularly the circadian relationship between the organism and the solar light-dark cycle.

12TRA asks two progressively more ambitious questions.

Stage I — Temporal Synchronization

What happens when human biological and psychological life becomes more consciously and behaviourally aligned with naturally recurring temporal cycles?

Stage II — Temporal Architecture

If synchronization is meaningful, do different phases within those cycles possess reproducible characteristics that can help humans understand, anticipate, coordinate, and intentionally work with change?

The distinction is fundamental.

Stage I investigates whether synchronization matters.

Stage II investigates whether synchronized time has structure.

This separation creates an evidentiary progression. The synchronization hypothesis can be investigated without accepting the archetypal model, while the psychological and social utility of temporal scaffolding can later be investigated independently of any proposed lunar or gravitational biological mechanism.

Stage I — Temporal Synchronization

Part I — The Foundations of Temporal Organization

2. Time as a Biological Phenomenon

Civil time is extraordinarily useful, but it is primarily a system of coordination.

Monday, September, 09:00, and the beginning of a financial quarter are human abstractions. Sunrise, darkness, the seasons, Earth's rotation, and the changing geometry of the Earth-Moon-Sun system are physical phenomena.

Biological organisms evolved within the latter.

Humans possess endogenous biological clocks, the most established being the circadian system. These clocks continuously establish relationships with environmental signals.

Living organisms therefore do not merely exist while time passes. Biological processes anticipate recurring environmental change.

This distinction is foundational to 12TRA:

Biological time is relational rather than merely chronological.

An organism repeatedly establishes phase relationships between its internal processes and periodic events in its environment.

3. The Hierarchy of Natural Cycles

Human beings exist within multiple nested cycles.

These include:

  • approximately 24-hour solar rhythms;
  • seasonal changes in photoperiod and environment;
  • approximately 29.5-day changes in lunar illumination;
  • approximately semilunar periodicities;
  • changing lunar altitude and moonrise/moonset timing;
  • Earth-Moon-Sun gravitational geometry;
  • tidal and gravimetric periodicities;
  • endogenous ultradian, circadian, and infradian biological rhythms.

The biological importance of these cycles is not equally established.

The solar/circadian relationship is strongly established in humans.

Seasonal effects are also biologically meaningful.

Lunar and especially ordinary terrestrial gravimetric effects on human physiology remain considerably less certain.

12TRA therefore treats these cycles as a hierarchy of research questions rather than assuming equivalent biological influence.

4. Three Domains of Human Time

There are at least three temporal domains relevant to human life.

Physical and Environmental Time

Recurring astronomical and environmental change:

  • solar day;
  • light and darkness;
  • seasons;
  • lunar illumination;
  • gravitational geometry;
  • temperature and other environmental cycles.

Biological Time

Endogenous oscillatory physiology:

  • circadian phase;
  • hormone rhythms;
  • temperature rhythms;
  • sleep propensity;
  • metabolic timing;
  • activity rhythms;
  • potentially longer-period biological oscillations.

Civil and Social Time

Human-created systems of coordination:

  • clocks;
  • time zones;
  • work schedules;
  • school schedules;
  • weekends;
  • calendar months;
  • deadlines.

These three domains can align.

They can also diverge.

12TRA begins with the proposition that the degree and nature of that divergence may matter.

Part II — The Theory of Temporal Dislocation

5. Temporal Dislocation

Temporal Dislocation is proposed as a research construct describing reduced coherence between biological, behavioural, psychological, social, and environmental temporal systems.

A working definition is:

Temporal Dislocation is a hypothesized state of reduced multiscale temporal coherence characterized by unstable or maladaptive phase relationships among endogenous biological rhythms, behavioural schedules, subjective temporal organization, and relevant environmental cycles.

Temporal Dislocation does not simply mean losing track of time.

A person can know the exact date and clock time while their sleep, endocrine rhythms, behavioural schedule, environmental exposure, and subjective experience of change remain poorly aligned.

Chronological accuracy is therefore not equivalent to temporal coherence.

Candidate levels of Temporal Dislocation

Circadian Dislocation

Mismatch involving approximately 24-hour biological organization.

Internal Temporal Dislocation

Desynchronization among endogenous systems, such as central circadian timing, peripheral metabolic rhythms, sleep, and feeding.

Social Temporal Dislocation

Mismatch between biological timing and institutionally imposed schedules.

Environmental Temporal Dislocation

Weak, contradictory, or artificially altered natural timing signals.

Infradian Temporal Dislocation

A proposed mismatch involving rhythms longer than approximately 24 hours.

Experiential Temporal Dislocation

A hypothesized psychological condition involving weak perception of transition, temporal fragmentation, or difficulty locating present experience within a larger trajectory of change.

These categories range from concepts closely related to established chronobiology to novel 12TRA hypotheses and should be investigated accordingly.

6. Regularity, Synchronization and Resonance

12TRA distinguishes several concepts that are often conflated.

Regularity

Repeated behaviour according to an external schedule.

Entrainment

Coupling between an endogenous oscillator and an external periodic signal.

Phase Alignment

An appropriate temporal relationship between oscillatory processes.

Temporal Coherence

Coordinated organization among multiple relevant rhythms.

Temporal Resonance

A proposed state in which endogenous and environmentally coupled temporal processes maintain stable and adaptive phase relationships across relevant timescales.

Temporal Resynchronization

The process by which disrupted temporal relationships return toward a more stable and adaptive configuration.

Regularity is therefore not necessarily resonance.

A person can maintain an extremely regular civil schedule while remaining biologically misaligned.

7. Modern Life and Temporal Dislocation

Modern civilization enables human behaviour to become increasingly independent of natural environmental timing.

Potential contributors include:

  • artificial light at night;
  • inadequate daylight exposure;
  • predominantly indoor environments;
  • shift work;
  • irregular sleep;
  • institutionally fixed schedules;
  • irregular feeding;
  • globally synchronized digital activity;
  • reduced seasonality;
  • civil calendar structures largely independent of astronomical cycles.

This does not imply that civil time is inherently harmful.

Civil time is essential infrastructure.

The scientific question is whether exclusive dependence on civil temporal organization can sometimes conflict with biological or environmental timing sufficiently to produce measurable consequences.

Part III — Physical Time and the Environment

8. The Solar Foundation

The strongest scientific foundation for Stage I is the relationship between the human circadian system and the solar light-dark environment.

Light is a major human zeitgeber.

The circadian system helps organize:

  • sleep and wakefulness;
  • endocrine activity;
  • body temperature;
  • metabolism;
  • cognition;
  • alertness;
  • numerous cellular processes.

Circadian misalignment demonstrates an important principle:

A human being can obey civil clock time while remaining biologically out of phase.

12TRA generalizes this established observation into the question of multiscale temporal coherence.

9. The Lunar Question

Lunar effects on humans require careful separation from folklore.

12TRA does not begin with the proposition:

The full moon controls human behaviour.

Instead it asks:

Do humans possess weak, variable, partially masked, or context-dependent biological or behavioural oscillations at approximately lunar or semilunar periods?

Lunar timing systems are well established in numerous non-human organisms.

Evidence concerning humans is more uncertain and requires rigorous longitudinal investigation.

The Moon is nevertheless scientifically interesting because it simultaneously provides:

  • a stable astronomical cycle;
  • changing nocturnal illumination;
  • changing Earth-Moon-Sun geometry;
  • predictable gravitational periodicities;
  • a directly observable approximately monthly environmental clock.

Human circalunar organization should therefore be treated as an open experimental question.

10. Gravity and the Rhythm of Change

Gravity occupies a special position in the original 12TRA theory.

Gravity is biologically relevant. Organisms evolved under Earth's gravitational environment, and mechanical forces influence biological systems.

The substantially more speculative question is whether subtle, ordinary variations associated with lunar and solar gravitational geometry provide meaningful timing information to human biology.

12TRA therefore separates three propositions:

Established

Gravity and mechanical loading affect biological systems.

Established in broader biology

Organisms can evolve timing systems associated with geophysical and tidal environments.

Unestablished in humans

Ordinary lunar gravimetric periodicity functions as a meaningful human zeitgeber.

The third proposition must remain an experimental hypothesis.

The larger 12TRA principle does not depend entirely upon it:

Physical cycles create recurring contexts of change, and biological organisms evolved mechanisms for anticipating recurring change.

Part IV — Temporal Synchronization

11. The Temporal Coherence Model

12TRA proposes viewing temporal organization as interacting layers:

Geophysical Oscillators
↓
solar / lunar / seasonal / gravitational periodicity

Environmental Signals
↓
light / darkness / temperature / activity opportunity / mechanical environment

Biological Oscillators
↓
circadian / ultradian / infradian / seasonal systems

Behavioural Rhythms
↓
sleep / feeding / movement / work / social activity

Cognitive Temporal Models
↓
anticipation / planning / memory / perceived transition

Subjective Experience and Identity Through Time

Feedback may occur between these layers.

The central research question is whether coherence across this architecture can itself become measurable.

12. Temporal Resynchronization

If Temporal Dislocation exists as a broader measurable construct, Temporal Resynchronization describes movement toward greater temporal coherence.

Potential components include:

  • stronger daytime light exposure;
  • appropriate darkness;
  • sleep alignment;
  • phase-appropriate activity;
  • feeding regularity and timing;
  • seasonal awareness;
  • natural environmental exposure;
  • awareness of longer natural cycles;
  • deliberate temporal orientation.

Some of these interventions are already supported by circadian science.

Others, particularly lunar orientation, remain experimental.

At its most conservative level, 12TRA can therefore be described as a:

Multiscale Temporal Orientation and Synchronization Architecture.

13. The Moon as a Shared Temporal Reference

The Moon has another property that does not depend on direct physiological entrainment.

It is an externally observable shared clock.

A natural lunar cycle provides a common reference through which individuals can identify not merely a date, but a position within a recurring cycle.

Two people can therefore share a synchronized temporal context.

This becomes important in Stage II because experiences separated by months or years can be compared according to equivalent positions within repeated cycles.

14. The Stage I Proposition

Stage I asks:

  1. Can Temporal Dislocation be operationally defined and measured?
  2. Can multiscale Temporal Coherence be quantified?
  3. Which environmental cycles measurably influence human temporal organization?
  4. Do meaningful human infradian rhythms exist?
  5. Do any maintain relationships with lunar or geophysical cycles?
  6. Can deliberate temporal orientation increase coherence?
  7. Does increased coherence correspond with measurable physiological, psychological, or behavioural benefits?

No archetypal hypothesis is necessary to answer these questions.

Stage I establishes whether there is a physical and biological foundation upon which Stage II can responsibly build.

Stage II — The Architecture of Change

Part V — From Synchronization to Temporal Structure

15. Does Synchronized Time Have Structure?

Suppose synchronization to a recurring natural cycle proves meaningful.

A second question follows:

Are all positions within that cycle functionally equivalent?

12TRA hypothesizes that recurring temporal positions may correspond with recurring contexts or processes of change.

Stage II therefore moves from temporal synchronization to temporal structure.

The question is no longer merely whether humans synchronize with time.

It becomes:

Does synchronized time possess a reproducible experiential architecture?

16. The Twelve Temporal Archetypes

12TRA divides the recurring lunar framework into twelve sequential temporal contexts represented by twelve canonical archetypes:

  1. Spark
  2. Analyst
  3. Connector
  4. Visionary
  5. Hearer
  6. Speaker
  7. Architect
  8. Integrator
  9. Strategist
  10. Evaluator
  11. Harmonizer
  12. Inspirer

Within the research architecture these should initially be treated as hypothesized temporal functions, not fixed personality categories.

A person is not necessarily "an Architect."

Instead, the hypothesis is that a particular temporal phase may preferentially contextualize processes associated with architecture, construction, ordering, or embodiment.

The archetypal model must remain corrigible.

Observed data must be allowed to refine, reorder, redefine, or reject elements of the proposed archetypal architecture.

17. The Functional Anatomy of Change

The twelve archetypes suggest a recurring sequence of functions involved in change:

Spark — initiation
Analyst — examination
Connector — relationship and association
Visionary — possibility and projection
Hearer — reception and listening
Speaker — articulation and expression
Architect — construction and structure
Integrator — synthesis and incorporation
Strategist — direction and planning
Evaluator — assessment and correction
Harmonizer — reconciliation and coherence
Inspirer — renewal and transmission

This sequence can be investigated as a proposed functional anatomy of change.

The research question is whether these functions appear with reproducible temporal structure rather than merely whether the symbolic framework is compelling.

Part VI — Mapping Human Experience Through Time

18. Temporal Context

Prediction is not the first objective of Stage II.

The first objective is context.

Human beings frequently experience emotional and psychological change without knowing where the experience lies within a larger trajectory.

A synchronized temporal architecture permits experiences to be indexed by recurring temporal position.

Over many cycles an individual can begin distinguishing:

  • personal patterns;
  • situational effects;
  • environmental effects;
  • recurrent temporal patterns;
  • random variation.

12TRA therefore begins as an observational instrument before becoming a predictive system.

19. Emotional Weather

One of the central Stage II concepts is Emotional Weather.

Weather provides an appropriate analogy because weather creates context without dictating behaviour.

A probability of rain does not force someone to become wet.

Likewise, a temporal phase associated statistically with a particular emotional tendency would not determine an individual's emotional state.

12TRA proposes investigating whether temporal phases exhibit reproducible tendencies in:

  • mood;
  • cognition;
  • motivation;
  • social orientation;
  • creativity;
  • receptivity;
  • expression;
  • evaluation;
  • conflict;
  • integration;
  • other dimensions of lived experience.

If sufficiently reproducible patterns exist, the system could eventually describe probabilistic emotional weather.

This must remain distinct from emotional determinism.

20. The Synchronized Time Machine

A conventional calendar primarily answers:

When am I?

A mature 12TRA system would attempt to answer:

Where am I within the cycle of change?

What happened during comparable positions in previous cycles?

What patterns are present now?

What normally follows?

This is the concept of the Synchronized Time Machine.

It is not physical time travel.

It is longitudinal navigation through synchronized experience.

An individual could move through records of equivalent temporal positions and compare:

  • emotional state;
  • relationships;
  • physiological measurements;
  • decisions;
  • events;
  • behavioural patterns;
  • changes initiated;
  • subsequent outcomes.

The past becomes structured comparative data.

The present acquires temporal context.

The future becomes increasingly susceptible to probabilistic anticipation.

21. Observe, Understand, Predict, Leverage

Stage II can be summarized as four progressively stronger operations.

Observe

Record experience against synchronized temporal position.

Understand

Identify recurring relationships and distinguish meaningful patterns from coincidence.

Predict

Where sufficiently replicated patterns exist, estimate probabilities of future states or contexts.

Leverage

Use validated temporal information when deciding when and how to initiate, develop, communicate, integrate, evaluate, or consolidate change.

This progression is fundamental:

Observe → Understand → Predict → Leverage

Prediction should never precede adequate observation.

Leverage should never precede reliable prediction or demonstrated utility.

Part VII — Working With Change

22. Temporal Leverage

If different temporal phases eventually demonstrate reproducible functional characteristics, individuals may be able to use temporal context deliberately.

Instead of treating every day as functionally interchangeable, activities could be aligned with contexts particularly suited to:

  • initiation;
  • investigation;
  • communication;
  • construction;
  • strategy;
  • evaluation;
  • integration;
  • reconciliation.

This does not imply inability to act outside an associated phase.

The analogy is sailing.

A vessel can travel against a current, but understanding the current changes how intelligently the journey can be planned.

12TRA calls the intentional use of validated temporal context Temporal Leverage.

23. Temporal Amplification

A stronger hypothesis follows:

Actions congruent with an existing temporal tendency may require less effort, achieve greater coherence, or produce more persistent change than equivalent actions undertaken without regard to temporal context.

This is the proposed Temporal Amplification effect.

Change could potentially be developed across successive phases:

initiate → examine → connect → envision → receive → articulate → construct → integrate → strategize → evaluate → harmonize → renew.

The lunar framework would then function not merely as a calendar but as a repeating architecture for deliberate change.

24. Personal Temporal Architecture

Longitudinal observation could eventually allow individuals to develop personalized temporal models.

A personal model could distinguish:

  • population-level temporal patterns;
  • individual recurring patterns;
  • environmental influences;
  • social effects;
  • unique personal responses.

The eventual objective would not be to force every person into an identical temporal profile.

Instead, 12TRA could provide a common coordinate system upon which individual variation is mapped.

This could lead to personalized emotional weather and individualized temporal forecasting.

Part VIII — Synchronizing People

25. Interpersonal Temporal Synchronization

Civil clocks allow people to synchronize events.

12TRA investigates whether a temporal architecture can help synchronize processes of change.

A couple, family, team, school, organization, or community could share a common understanding of where they are within a longer process.

Instead of merely agreeing:

"We meet Tuesday at 10."

a group could eventually share an additional context:

"This is the evaluative portion of our current cycle; examination and correction are deliberately emphasized before we move toward integration and harmonization."

This provides shared temporal semantics.

26. Synchronizing Change Between People

Interpersonal synchronization could involve coordinated periods of:

  • initiation;
  • exploration;
  • listening;
  • communication;
  • construction;
  • integration;
  • strategic planning;
  • evaluation;
  • reconciliation;
  • renewal.

The hypothesis is that some interpersonal friction may arise not simply from disagreement but from people attempting incompatible change processes simultaneously.

A shared temporal framework could make those differences observable and potentially easier to coordinate.

27. Collective Emotional Weather

If individual emotional or behavioural tendencies exhibit temporal structure, a further question is whether groups exhibit emergent temporal patterns.

Potential subjects include:

  • classrooms;
  • families;
  • teams;
  • organizations;
  • communities.

Collective Emotional Weather would refer to statistically observable group-level tendencies associated with temporal context.

This is a substantially stronger claim than individual temporal scaffolding and would require correspondingly stronger evidence.

It also introduces significant ethical issues concerning prediction, labeling, institutional power, and autonomy.

Part IX — Applications

28. 12TRA in Education

Schools provide a particularly interesting environment for studying collective synchronization.

Conventional timetables synchronize activity:

  • mathematics at a particular hour;
  • language at another;
  • breaks at defined intervals.

12TRA would explore whether longer developmental processes could also be synchronized.

A learning community might emphasize, at different points:

  • curiosity and initiation;
  • investigation;
  • collaboration;
  • imagination;
  • listening;
  • presentation;
  • construction;
  • synthesis;
  • planning;
  • critique;
  • reconciliation;
  • renewed inspiration.

The curriculum need not change.

The temporal emphasis changes.

A school could therefore become an environment for testing whether shared temporal scaffolding improves learning, reflection, cooperation, creativity, or wellbeing.

A shared temporal vocabulary could provide groups with a structured language for:

what is happening from where the group is within a process.

This may eventually allow groups to coordinate change rather than merely coordinate meetings.

Part X — Scientific Architecture

32. The Evidence Ladder

12TRA contains claims with very different levels of evidentiary support.

They should never be presented as though they are equally established.

A useful evidence ladder is:

  1. Circadian entrainment and biological timing — strongly established.
  2. Circadian misalignment and social temporal conflict — established.
  3. Broader multiscale Temporal Dislocation — proposed construct.
  4. Meaningful human infradian temporal organization — research question.
  5. Human lunar entrainment — emerging and unresolved hypothesis.
  6. Human gravimetric lunar entrainment — highly exploratory hypothesis.
  7. Recurring temporal phenomenology — Stage II hypothesis.
  8. Twelve archetypal temporal functions — 12TRA-specific hypothesis.
  9. Emotional Weather — predictive hypothesis.
  10. Temporal Amplification — intervention hypothesis.
  11. Interpersonal and collective synchronization — social application hypothesis.

The research programme should move upward through this ladder rather than assuming the highest claims from the beginning.

33. Three Levels of Stage II Evidence

Stage II can itself be separated into three levels.

Level A — Temporal Scaffolding

Using the twelve-phase architecture improves planning, reflection, communication, behaviour, or group coordination.

This could operate entirely through cognitive and social mechanisms.

Level B — Recurring Phenomenology

Particular temporal phases demonstrate reproducible emotional, cognitive, or behavioural tendencies.

This would support Emotional Weather.

Level C — Biological Correspondence

Those recurring phenomenological phases correspond with reproducible physiological rhythms associated with the underlying natural cycle.

Each level can succeed or fail independently.

Level A may remain useful even if B and C fail.

Level B could exist without the proposed mechanism in C.

This structure allows the theory to be progressively refined rather than protected from falsification.

34. Falsifiability and Scientific Boundaries

12TRA must be structured so that its hypotheses can fail.

Examples include:

  • no reproducible infradian rhythms;
  • infradian rhythms that do not correlate with lunar cycles;
  • apparent lunar effects explained entirely by light exposure;
  • no gravimetric association after controlling for environmental variables;
  • no reproducible archetypal phenomenology;
  • 12TRA scheduling performing no better than conventional structured planning;
  • subjective benefits occurring without physiological change;
  • physiological effects occurring without archetypal correspondence.

Negative findings should alter the architecture.

They should not be explained away.

This principle is essential if 12TRA is to develop as a research programme rather than a belief system.

35. Ethics and Responsible Application

Potential predictive and group applications require explicit safeguards.

12TRA should avoid:

  • temporal determinism;
  • diagnosing people according to temporal phase;
  • treating archetypes as immutable identities;
  • replacing established medical or psychological care;
  • coercive institutional scheduling;
  • labeling children according to predicted emotional states;
  • using group predictions to suppress individual autonomy;
  • inappropriate collection of physiological or emotional data.

A temporal forecast should function like weather information:

context for decision-making, not authority over the individual.

Part XI — Research Vision

36. Stage I Research Objective: Synchronizing the Organism With Time

Stage I investigates whether humans can be more coherently synchronized with natural temporal structure.

Its central concepts are:

  • Temporal Dislocation;
  • entrainment;
  • phase alignment;
  • Temporal Coherence;
  • environmental timing;
  • circadian synchronization;
  • infradian rhythms;
  • lunar timing;
  • gravimetric hypotheses;
  • Temporal Resynchronization.

The foundational question is:

Can measurable relationships between natural temporal structure and human biological, behavioural, or psychological organization be identified and deliberately strengthened?

37. Stage II Research Objective: Synchronizing Change Within Time

Stage II asks whether synchronized temporal position provides meaningful information about the processes of change.

Its central concepts are:

  • Temporal Archetypes;
  • Temporal Context;
  • Emotional Weather;
  • longitudinal comparison;
  • prediction;
  • Temporal Leverage;
  • Temporal Amplification;
  • interpersonal synchronization.

The foundational question becomes:

If humans share a synchronized temporal reference, can recurring biological, emotional, psychological, and interpersonal processes be mapped within it and eventually used to understand, anticipate, and coordinate change?

38. From Individual to Collective Temporal Coherence

The architecture can be investigated at progressively larger scales:

Individual

Biological and psychological temporal organization.

Relationship

Synchronization between two people.

Family

Shared cycles of communication and change.

School

Collective learning and developmental rhythms.

Organization

Project, strategic, and interpersonal processes.

Community

Large-scale shared temporal context.

Movement between these levels should occur only when evidence supports it.

39. The Long-Term 12TRA Vision

The long-term proposition of 12TRA is larger than the creation of another calendar.

Civil calendars synchronize civilization around dates.

Circadian science helps synchronize organisms around daily biological time.

12TRA asks whether a multiscale architecture could eventually synchronize people around change itself.

The conceptual progression is:

Physical Cycles
↓
Biological Synchronization
↓
Temporal Awareness
↓
Mapping Recurring Experience
↓
Emotional and Psychological Weather
↓
Anticipation
↓
Intentional Amplification of Change
↓
Interpersonal Synchronization
↓
Collective Temporal Coherence

At its most conservative, 12TRA is a proposal for investigating multiscale human temporal synchronization.

At its intermediate level, it becomes a framework for observing recurring biological and psychological change.

At its strongest experimentally supported form, it could become a shared system through which individuals and groups understand, anticipate, coordinate, and deliberately work with change.

Conclusion

40. Toward a Science of Temporal Resonance

The central proposition of 12TRA can be stated simply:

12TRA investigates whether human beings can first become more synchronized with the natural architecture of time, and then use that synchronization to observe, understand, anticipate, and intentionally coordinate the processes of change occurring within themselves and between one another.

The research programme therefore contains two distinct stages.

Stage I asks whether the clock is real.

Can Temporal Dislocation be measured?

Can natural temporal synchronization increase biological, behavioural, or psychological coherence?

Which physical cycles matter?

Stage II asks whether the clock has weather.

Do positions within synchronized time correspond with recurring physiological, emotional, psychological, or interpersonal contexts?

Can those patterns be observed reliably?

Can they eventually be predicted?

And only after establishing both does 12TRA ask its most ambitious question:

Can humans learn to sail that weather?

Appendix G — Research Questions and Falsification Criteria

Core questions include:

  1. Does measurable multiscale Temporal Dislocation exist?
  2. Can Temporal Coherence be operationalized?
  3. Which environmental cycles contribute to it?
  4. Are reproducible human infradian rhythms detectable?
  5. Do any correspond with lunar periodicities?
  6. Can moonlight and gravitational hypotheses be experimentally distinguished?
  7. Does natural-cycle temporal orientation improve outcomes?
  8. Do recurring temporal phases exhibit reproducible phenomenology?
  9. Does the twelve-archetype model accurately describe those patterns?
  10. Can Emotional Weather be predicted above chance?
  11. Does phase-congruent action produce Temporal Amplification?
  12. Can shared temporal architecture improve interpersonal or collective coordination?

Every question must permit a negative result.
